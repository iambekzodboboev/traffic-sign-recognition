The route from photo to report

Four stops, in a fixed order.

Each stage's output is exactly what the next stage needs — a photo becomes a crop, a crop becomes a class, a class becomes a line in a trip report.

01

Photo or driving video

A single sign photo, or an uploaded/linked driving clip — 200 real sign classes, 116,642 training images sourced from a Kaggle post-Soviet-states dataset.

input
02

Detect & crop

Classical color-and-shape detection in OpenCV finds candidate sign regions in each sampled video frame — chosen over a downloaded pretrained detector after a direct test: official COCO-pretrained YOLOv8 missed a clearly visible STOP sign that the classical approach caught on the first try.

no external model
03

ResNet18 classifier

A pretrained ResNet18, fully fine-tuned, predicts the sign type and category for every crop. Swapping in this backbone — with everything else in the pipeline held identical — was the single change that took accuracy from 87.84% to 99.37%.

transfer learning
04

Tracked trip report

Detections are matched across sampled frames so the same physical sign isn't reported twice, then rolled up into a named, timestamped, confidence-scored list — click any entry and the source video jumps straight to that moment.

output

The gain wasn't a generic bump — specific baseline confusions (e.g. mirrored left/right minor-road signs, confused 92 times) dropped to zero once the backbone changed, which is harder to explain away as overfitting than a uniform accuracy increase would be.

Stated, not hidden

Where it still breaks.

!

Camera-mount assumption in the video detector

The detector's sign-search region was built and verified against two dashboard-mounted test clips; a fixed-fraction assumption in the code means a very different camera angle — a much higher windshield mount, or landscape video — hasn't yet been confirmed to work as well. A scoped fix is already written up, not just noted: read the feature specification.

Two more, found by direct testing on real footage: two sign faces stacked on one post can be boxed together and confidently mislabeled as something unrelated (not just flagged low-confidence); and confidence itself reads as a weaker trust signal on small, blurry video crops than on the large clean photos the classifier was validated on.
