01 · Context

The project in one clear story.

Recognizing an isolated, cropped sign photo is the easy version of this problem — real driving footage buries each sign in a moving, cluttered scene.

The problem

A classifier trained on clean, sign-only photos performs very differently on a real driving scene, where the sign is a small, moving detail in a busy frame — confirmed directly in this project when a wide street photo was misread by the first deployed version.

Who uses it

Driving students and instructors reviewing a practice drive, or anyone curious what signs actually appear in a piece of driving footage.

02 · System

How it works, without the notebook noise.

Four stages, from a raw photo or video to a named, timestamped result.

Photo or video

A single sign photo, or an uploaded/linked driving video — 200 real sign classes, 116,642 training images.

Detect & crop

Classical color + shape detection (OpenCV) finds candidate sign regions in each sampled video frame — no external detector model needed.

ResNet18 classifier

A pretrained ResNet18, fine-tuned on the full dataset, predicts the sign type and category for each crop.

Tracked trip report

Detections are matched across frames so the same physical sign isn't reported twice — the result is a named, timestamped, confidence-scored list.

What this proves

Consistent across all 200 classesMacro-F1 sits almost exactly at overall accuracy, not propped up by a few frequent classes.
Tested on real driving video, not just clean photosThe video pipeline ran against two different real driving videos, with its failure modes documented honestly, not only its successes.

Known limitation

The video detector's sign-search region was built and tested against two specific dashboard-mounted camera clips; a documented fixed-region assumption in the code means a very different camera angle or mount (e.g. a much higher windshield mount, or landscape video) hasn't yet been verified to work as well. This is a known, stated scope boundary, not a hidden one — see the project's own feature specification for the planned fix.
